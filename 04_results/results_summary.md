---

## Key Findings

### PiZ Documented Mutation
- **Nucleotide change**: c.1096 G → A
- **Codon change**: `GAG` → `AAG` at codon 366
- **Amino acid change**: Glutamic acid (negative) → Lysine (positive)
- **Effect**: Single amino acid substitution — protein length unchanged, reading frame preserved. Charge change disrupts folding → abnormal polymerization → retained in hepatocytes → not secreted.

### 1-bp Deletion Artificial Mutation
- **Nucleotide change**: c.298 delete single C
- **Sequence change**: `ACCCTTT` → `ACCTTT`
- **Effect**: Reading frame shifts at codon 100. All downstream codons rewritten → premature stop at ~aa 123 → protein truncated by ~295 residues. Entire C-terminal functional domain lost.

---

## Conclusion

Both mutations alter the *SERPINA1* sequence and reduce functional α₁-antitrypsin, but through very different mechanisms:

- **PiZ missense** changes one amino acid while keeping length and frame intact — protein is produced but misfolds, causing partial deficiency.
- **1-bp deletion frameshift** rewrites the sequence from position 100 and stops early — protein is drastically truncated and completely non-functional.

Frameshift mutations (deletions not divisible by 3) are far more disruptive than single missense substitutions because they alter every downstream amino acid and often introduce premature termination.
